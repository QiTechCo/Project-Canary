# Three Reflections from This Week at City Council

**Published:** Tue, 23 Jun 2026 19:58:42 GMT  
**Direct Link:** [https://dimpleajmera.substack.com/p/three-reflections-from-this-week](https://dimpleajmera.substack.com/p/three-reflections-from-this-week)

---

This week, City Council made decisions that will impact public safety and the future of our city, while also marking the end of an important chapter in Charlotte’s leadership.

### Safer Streets: Red-Light Camera Pilot Approved
Council approved a red-light camera pilot program at some of Charlotte’s most crash-prone intersections. This is about one thing: **saving lives**.

The goal isn’t writing tickets. The goal is changing behavior. If drivers slow down and stop running red lights, then this program is doing exactly what it’s supposed to do.

Success will be measured by fewer crashes, fewer injuries, and more families making it home safely. Thank you to Sustain Charlotte and the many advocates who have kept the focus on safer streets and protecting human life.

### Leadership Transition: Welcoming Interim Mayor Rob Harrington
This week, Council appointed Rob Harrington as Charlotte’s interim mayor.

This was one of the most difficult decisions I’ve made in public service because we had an extraordinary pool of applicants and many highly qualified candidates. I spent a great deal of time reflecting on this decision because it impacts every neighborhood in our city.

Mr. Harrington brings decades of civic leadership and community engagement to this role, and I look forward to working collaboratively with him on the issues that matter most to Charlotte residents, including public safety, transportation, affordability, and managing growth responsibly.

### Thank You, Mayor Vi Lyles
I also want to thank Mayor Vi Lyles for her years of service to Charlotte.

While we have not always agreed on policy, I respect her commitment to public service and recognize the sacrifices that public office places not only on elected officials but also on their families. Public service is a family commitment, and those sacrifices often happen away from the public eye.

I wish Mayor Lyles and her family all the best in this next chapter.

As always, thank you for the opportunity to serve our city. Charlotte’s best days are ahead, and I remain committed to working together to build a safer, stronger, and more united community.
