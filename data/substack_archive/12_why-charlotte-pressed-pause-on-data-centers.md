# Why Charlotte Pressed Pause on Data Centers

**Published:** Tue, 09 Jun 2026 17:01:17 GMT  
**Direct Link:** [https://dimpleajmera.substack.com/p/why-charlotte-pressed-pause-on-data](https://dimpleajmera.substack.com/p/why-charlotte-pressed-pause-on-data)

Friends,

Yesterday, the City Council made two important decisions that will shape Charlotte’s future for years to come.

The most significant was our unanimous vote to approve a 150-day pause on new data center applications.

This issue did not emerge overnight.

Over the past several months, I began raising concerns about whether Charlotte was prepared for the rapid growth of data centers and whether our existing policies adequately addressed their potential impacts on infrastructure, utilities, land use, and surrounding communities.

As a city, we have a responsibility to welcome innovation and economic opportunity. But we also have a responsibility to understand the long-term consequences of our decisions before they become difficult—or impossible—to reverse.

That is why I called for Charlotte to take a step back and carefully evaluate this issue before moving forward.

Yesterday, the City Council unanimously agreed.

## Why I Called for a Pause
Let me be clear: this was never about being against technology, innovation, or economic development.

The question before us was not whether data centers are good or bad.

The question was whether Charlotte has the right policy framework in place to manage them responsibly.

My concern was straightforward.

Data centers are unlike traditional office buildings or commercial developments. They can have substantial impacts on energy demand, water consumption, infrastructure capacity, and surrounding land uses. Across the country, local governments are wrestling with these challenges after projects have already been approved.

Charlotte has the opportunity to learn from those experiences instead of repeating them.

As I shared during Council’s discussion:

“The question before us is not whether data centers are good or bad. The question is whether Charlotte has the right policy framework in place to manage them responsibly.”

Today, I believe the answer is that we need to do our homework.

A 150-day pause gives us time to do exactly that.

It allows city staff to evaluate best practices, study infrastructure impacts, engage stakeholders, and recommend policies that ensure future decisions are informed by facts and data.

As I said from the dais:

“A 150-day pause today can prevent years of unintended consequences tomorrow.”

Good government is not about saying yes to every opportunity. Nor is it about saying no.

Good government is about asking the right questions before making decisions that affect taxpayers, neighborhoods, and future generations.

That is what this pause allows us to do.

And I am proud that the Council ultimately came together unanimously in support of that approach.
