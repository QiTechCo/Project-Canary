# Join Me on June 8: Let’s Get Data Center Regulations Right

**Published:** Sun, 07 Jun 2026 16:44:53 GMT  
**Direct Link:** [https://dimpleajmera.substack.com/p/join-me-on-june-8-lets-get-data-center](https://dimpleajmera.substack.com/p/join-me-on-june-8-lets-get-data-center)

---

Friends,<br><br>On Monday, June 8, the Charlotte City Council will vote on a proposal to temporarily pause new data center approvals while we develop thoughtful regulations and guardrails.<br><br>I am asking you to show up and make your voice heard.<br><br>This conversation is not about being anti-growth, anti-business, or anti-technology. Charlotte has always embraced innovation, and we should continue to do so. But we also have a responsibility to ensure that growth happens responsibly and that residents are not left bearing the consequences.<br><br>Over the past several months, I have heard from residents across Charlotte who have raised concerns about:<br><br>• Data centers being proposed closer and closer to residential neighborhoods<br>• Noise from 24/7 operations and backup generators<br>• Massive electricity and water demands during a period of growing resource constraints<br>• Environmental and quality-of-life impacts on surrounding communities<br>• The lack of public input when some projects can be approved “by right” under current zoning rules<br><br>Many residents are surprised to learn that data centers are currently allowed by right in nine zoning districts, including some mixed-use districts and Uptown. That means certain projects can move forward without a public hearing, without a community meeting, and without a vote from City Council.<br><br>That should concern all of us.<br><br>One resident recently shared with me his concerns about a proposed data center near his neighborhood while raising a child with autism. He worried about the constant industrial noise and how it could affect his family’s quality of life. Whether you agree or disagree with him, his concerns deserve to be heard.<br><br>A temporary pause gives us the opportunity to answer important questions, study the impacts, engage stakeholders, and create modern regulations before additional projects move forward.<br><br>This is a nonpartisan issue. Communities across North Carolina and across the country are grappling with these same questions. Residents deserve transparency, public engagement, and a seat at the table.<br><br>If you haven’t had a chance to read my recent Charlotte Observer op-ed on this issue, you can find it here:<br><br>“Charlotte Needs to Answer Real Concerns About Data Centers”<br>Read More: https://www.charlotteobserver.com/opinion/article315470507.html<br><br>I hope you’ll join us Monday evening.<br><br>Charlotte-Mecklenburg Government Center<br>600 E. Fourth Street

Monday, June 8<br>Meeting begins at 5:00 PM<br><br>Charlotte’s future should be shaped with residents, not around them.<br><br>I hope to see you there.
